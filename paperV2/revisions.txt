Reviewer 1

This manuscript introduces a steganographic method for hiding synthetic cryptographic artifacts within language model generated text. By converting payloads like hashes or nonces into bounded token ranks, the approach generates cover text under a shared prompt. The method explores direct subword and bounded encodings across segmented and non segmented configurations. Recovery depends on an exact copy replay condition using the identical model and prompt configuration.

Overall, this manuscript presents an interesting mechanism for rank based steganography. The writing is coherent, and the foundational concept is well articulated. However, the manuscript currently exhibits significant experimental design limitations that should be addressed. The sample size is insufficient to draw robust conclusions. Furthermore, the evaluation lacks human readability assessments and relies on a preliminary baseline for detectability. The methodology also appears fragile due to the strict exact copy requirement. Because these technical and experimental deficiencies require extensive additional validation to prove practical viability, my recommendation is Major Revision.

1). In the Methods section, the exact copy replay assumption is highly restrictive. This fragility limits practical application in real communication channels. I suggest adding a theoretical discussion or experimental analysis on mitigating minor formatting disruptions during transmission.
2). In the Evaluation corpus section, using only two examples per payload class is statistically insufficient. This small sample size prevents reliable conclusions. I suggest expanding the dataset to include a significantly larger number of diverse examples per class.
3). In the Results section, the cover text quality is measured solely by log probability proxies. This does not accurately reflect human perceived naturalness. I suggest incorporating a human evaluation study or established readability metrics to validate text fluency.
4). In the Methods section, the deterministic token filter is mentioned but its impact is not systematically isolated. This leaves the filter effectiveness somewhat unclear. I suggest adding an ablation study comparing generation quality with and without the filter.
5). In the Results section, the single experimental lead in variant failed exact recovery. This indicates potential boundary alignment vulnerabilities. I suggest conducting a dedicated experiment to test various lead in lengths and analyzing the specific causes of these failures.
6). In the Segmented multi cover method section, the greedy natural tail policy relies on arbitrary length caps. This cutoff might introduce unnatural sentence endings. I suggest implementing and evaluating dynamic stopping criteria based on semantic completeness instead.
7). In the Discussion section, the paper claims defensive value for detecting hidden encodings but provides a preliminary detector baseline. The steganalysis appears incomplete. I suggest implementing state of the art neural steganalysis models to evaluate the empirical detectability.
8). In the Results section, the performance difference between single topic and multi topic prompts lacks statistical validation. The topic impact remains ambiguous. I suggest performing significance testing across a broader range of prompt categories to verify the topic effects.
9). In the Payload representation section, the capacity quality tradeoff is visualized but not mathematically formalized. The relationship remains purely empirical. I suggest providing a theoretical model that defines the expected tradeoff bounds between payload capacity and cover length.
10). In the Evaluation corpus section, the base64 ciphertext like strings are not generated by real encryption algorithms. This limits the realism of the payload simulation. I suggest using outputs from standard encryption protocols to ensure realistic payload distributions.
11). In the Methods section, the dependency on a single language model architecture limits the generalizability of the findings. The method robustness is uncertain. I suggest replicating the core experiments using at least one additional open source language model architecture.
12). In the Discussion section, the computational overhead of the encoding and decoding processes is not thoroughly discussed. The practical efficiency remains unclear. I suggest adding a performance analysis detailing the time and memory costs associated with the RankCloak operations.
13). In the reference list, the manuscript relies heavily on unrefereed arXiv preprints (e.g., References 1, 12, 28, 32, 34) and technical reports. This reliance weakens the scholarly rigor. I suggest updating these citations to their peer-reviewed versions if published, or minimizing their overall proportion.

Reviewer 2

This paper proposes RankCloak, an LLM-based rank-transcoding steganography framework for hiding synthetic cryptographic artifacts (e.g., hashes, UUIDs, ciphertext-like strings) inside natural language text. It explores different payload representations (direct subword, ASCII-byte, hex-nibble), segmentation strategies, and natural-tail generation under exact-copy replay settings. The study demonstrates that bounded-rank encoding can improve controllability of hidden payloads while revealing a clear trade-off between payload compactness, cover quality, and recoverability.

# Pros:
1) The paper carefully separates payload representation, recovery correctness, and cover quality, providing a useful framework for studying LLM-based steganography.
2) The comparison of direct tokenization, bounded codecs, and segmented generation provides valuable insights into the capacity–quality trade-off of rank-based hiding.
3) The authors clearly define assumptions, replay conditions, codecs, metrics, and failure cases instead of overstating security claims.

# Cons:
1) Experiments are mainly conducted under a controlled exact-copy replay setting with synthetic payloads and a limited evaluation corpus. The paper does not sufficiently investigate larger-scale LLMs, different model families, multilingual settings, or real-world deployment scenarios. More comprehensive experiments across various LLM architectures and generation settings are needed.
2) The paper mainly relies on model-based metrics (e.g., token log probability and artifact flags), while human evaluation and stronger steganalysis baselines are missing. Since the core claim concerns hiding artifacts in “plain English”, human perception studies and comparisons with existing linguistic steganography methods are necessary. The authors may consider discussing and comparing with prior linguistic steganography approaches such as Ding et al., “Self-Attention with Cross-Lingual Position Representation” for broader representation learning perspectives, although the connection should be clarified.
