# Results handoff for manuscript revision

The source corpus contained 15,840 detector observations. One normalized exact-duplicate group involved 2 rows; removing one complete matched pair left 15,838 observations. The audit found 52 remaining pairs at cosine similarity at least 0.95, including 44 across payload groups. Component-level assignment yielded 9,904 train, 2,952 validation, and 2,982 test observations with zero audited cross-partition links. No model, codec, payload-class, or template condition became unavailable.

On the matched LLM-control test, TextCNN ROC-AUC was 0.9868 (95% CI 0.9819--0.9913) and normalized 0--1% pAUC was 0.6652. DeBERTa ROC-AUC was 0.9994 (95% CI 0.9984--1.0000) and pAUC was 0.9948. The model-aware surprisal detector reached ROC-AUC 0.999995 and pAUC 0.999460, showing that an attacker with exact-model token log probabilities has a substantially stronger signal than text alone.

Leave-one-family-out TextCNN ROC-AUC ranged from 0.9186 to 0.9784; DeBERTa ranged from 0.9972 to 1.0000; and the model-aware detector ranged from 0.999943 to 1.000000. These are genuine family holdouts on deduplicated test components, not random-split estimates.

At validation-selected 1%-FPR thresholds, matched-test TPRs were 0.8317 for TextCNN, 0.9913 for DeBERTa, and 0.9987 for the model-aware detector. The corresponding observed test FPRs were 0.0127, 0.0007, and 0.0020; a frozen validation threshold need not reproduce the target FPR in test. The matched test had 1491 negatives and supported the 0.1% estimand. Each family holdout had fewer than 1,000 validation or test negatives, so 0.1% TPR is unavailable there rather than interpolated.

Across family holdouts, the observed FPR at the frozen validation 1% threshold ranged from 0.0201 to 0.0704 for TextCNN, 0.0040 to 0.0563 for DeBERTa, and 0.0000 to 0.0060 for the model-aware detector. The maxima were 35/497, 28/497, and 3/497 false positives, respectively. Thus high held-family ROC-AUC did not guarantee calibration transfer at a nominal low-FPR threshold.

The deterministic Dolly pipeline selected 250 human-authored controls spanning 16 templates. It found no normalized exact duplicates, no within-human near-duplicate pairs, and no near-duplicate links to the primary corpus. TextCNN produced RankCloak-versus-human ROC-AUC 0.7537 (95% CI 0.7141--0.7918); at its LLM-validation 1%-FPR threshold, 152/250 human controls were false positives (empirical FPR 0.6080, grouped-bootstrap 95% CI 0.5469--0.6667). DeBERTa-v3-base produced RankCloak-versus-human ROC-AUC 0.9846 (95% CI 0.9771--0.9910); at its LLM-validation 1%-FPR threshold, 97/250 human controls were false positives (empirical FPR 0.3880, grouped-bootstrap 95% CI 0.3263--0.4466). These comparisons are secondary because they can reflect general machine-versus-human distinctions.

In the bounded 144-trial replay-mode robustness sample, saved-token-ID recovery succeeded for 144/144 trials (rate 1.0000, Wilson 95% CI 0.9740--1.0000), whereas unmodified visible text after detokenization and retokenization recovered 88/144 (rate 0.6111, 95% CI 0.5296--0.6869). Greedy lead-in regeneration recovered 144/144. Thus exact saved-ID replay and visible-text recovery are empirically distinct modes in this sample.

All 720 paired single-topic versus multi-topic outputs were distinct. Mean normalized character edit distance was 0.6179 (payload-group bootstrap 95% CI 0.6070--0.6287); mean word-type Jaccard similarity was 0.2531 (95% CI 0.2441--0.2623). Saved-ID exact recovery was 720/720 for single-topic and 720/720 for multi-topic trials. Visible-text recovery was not executed for these paired records and is unavailable.

No model-backed entropy or matched-quantization outcome was produced. The fail-closed dry-run enumerated 720 new entropy-study evaluation generations plus 18 calibration traces, and 3840 quantization analysis rows comprising 1920 reusable Q4 rows and 1920 planned Q8 rows. Preflight status was blocked; these counts are plans, not experimental results.
