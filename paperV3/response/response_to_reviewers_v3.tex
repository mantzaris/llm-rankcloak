\subsection*{Practical interpretation and requested evaluations}

\begin{reviewcomment}
\textquotedblleft The manuscript has been substantially improved, and its systematic evaluation, reproducibility, and transparent reporting are appreciated. However, major concerns remain regarding the practical interpretation of RankCloak. Perfect recovery depends on saved-token replay, while visible-text recovery is limited and robustness under paraphrasing, format transfer, and cross-model decoding remains poor. In addition, the extremely high steganalysis performance requires stronger evaluation using human-written controls, unseen models and quantizations, adaptive detectors, low-FPR operating points, and strictly deduplicated training data. Entropy-aware embedding strategies should also be experimentally investigated. Suggested references are optional, and the authors may select appropriate recent literature. Reviewer comments should be preserved accurately in future revisions.\textquotedblright
-Chun-Wei Yang
\end{reviewcomment}

\noindent\textbf{Response.}
We agree with the practical boundary. The revised title and interpretation present RankCloak primarily as a measurement and evaluation framework. Saved-ID recovery is described as deterministic replay requiring the token IDs, exact model, tokenizer, quantization, prompt rendering, filters, and span boundaries; it is materially different from communication through ordinary visible text. The 88/144 visible-text recovery result and zero recovery under markdown transfer, paraphrase, and cross-model decoding remain prominent.

The new bounded evaluation adds 250 human-authored Dolly controls as a secondary corpus comparison, not a human study; a complete pre-feature exact/near-duplicate component split with a zero-linkage audit; genuine leave-one-family-out tests; two data-adaptive text-only detectors; an exact-model-aware surprisal attacker; and validation-frozen 1\% and, only where sample size permits, 0.1\% operating points. It also adds entropy-gated embedding and matched Qwen Q4\_K\_M/Q8\_0 generation, recovery, and detector transfer. Strict entropy gating lowers text-only AUC in the tested matrix but lowers capacity, increases length, causes six completion failures, and leaves model-aware AUC 0.9904. Cross-quantization detection remains very strong. These results narrow rather than eliminate the concerns.

\subsection*{Concern 2: Controls, unseen conditions, adaptive detection, low FPR, and deduplication}

\begin{reviewcomment}
2) Detectability is devastatingly high (ROC-AUC 0.990/0.9998), yet controls are model-generated rather than human communication. Evaluation should include human text, unseen model families and quantizations, adaptive attackers, low-FPR operating points, and detector training on strictly deduplicated corpora.
\end{reviewcomment}

\Status{Addressed with bounded evaluations; generalization and threat-model limits remain}

\noindent\textbf{Response.}
We rebuilt the detector corpus before feature extraction. NFKC-normalized, case-folded, whitespace-collapsed exact duplicates triggered removal of a complete matched pair. Payload groups connected by any predeclared 0.95 character-\(n\)-gram TF-IDF near-duplicate edge became indivisible components. The retained 15,838 rows were assigned 9,904/2,952/2,982 to train/validation/test, and the audit found zero cross-partition payload, pair, normalized-text, component, or declared near-duplicate links.

Matched ROC-AUC is 0.9868 for TextCNN, 0.9994 for DeBERTa-v3-base, and 0.999995 for the exact-model-aware surprisal attacker. Genuine leave-one-family-out ROC-AUC ranges are 0.9186--0.9784, 0.9972--1.0000, and 0.999943--1.000000. The 250 human-authored controls yield ROC-AUC 0.7537 and 0.9846, but at thresholds selected on ordinary-model validation controls, 152/250 and 97/250 human texts are false positives. We therefore present this as a secondary corpus analysis that can reflect generic machine-versus-human signal, not as a participant study or naturalness evidence.

Low-FPR thresholds are selected on validation and frozen before test, with exact counts and no interpolation. The matched split has 1,491 test negatives and supports 0.1\% reporting. Family holdouts, human controls, entropy gates, and quantization tests have fewer than 1,000 negatives, so their empirical 0.1\% results are explicitly unavailable. Bidirectional Q4/Q8 detector transfer remains very strong; frozen 1\% thresholds show calibration drift in several tests. These adaptive and model-aware evaluations remain bounded rather than exhaustive.

\Locations{Main manuscript detector Methods, pp.~11--12; Figure~5, p.~18; Table~3, p.~19; Discussion, pp.~19--20; limitations, p.~20; Supplementary Tables S18--S21, pp.~20--21; Figures S8--S9, pp.~22--23; Note S12, pp.~20--23; Note S14, pp.~26--29.}

\subsection*{Concern 3: Entropy-aware embedding}

\begin{reviewcomment}
3) Entropy-aware token selection is especially relevant because RankCloak forces unlikely tokens and is readily detected. The authors should discuss and experimentally compare entropy-gated embedding, citing Cai et al., \textquotedblleft Entropy-Guided Watermarking for LLMs: A Test-Time Framework for Robust and Traceable Text Generation,\textquotedblright\ while clearly distinguishing watermark detection from payload-carrying steganography.
\end{reviewcomment}

\Status{Addressed experimentally within a bounded matrix}

\noindent\textbf{Response.}
We added the verified Cai, Ding, and Tao reference (arXiv:2504.12108; DOI \url{https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2504.12108}) and distinguish the tasks explicitly: watermarking asks a detector to identify marked output, whereas payload-carrying steganography requires a receiver to recover an arbitrary message.

The new replay-consistent experiment compares ungated, model-specific median-entropy, and 75th-percentile strict gates, with 120 RankCloak attempts and 120 length-matched controls per gate. Ineligible positions use ordinary top-\(p\) sampling and consume no payload rank; replay recomputes the gate without saved gate-position metadata. Strict gating completed 114/120 payloads, with six maximum-budget failures. Mean capacity fell from 7.4001 ungated to 4.0513 bits/token under the strict gate, and the latter is explicitly conditional on the 114 completed cases. Mean length rose 2.7615-fold. Visible-text recovery was 62/120, 65/120, and 68/120 across ungated, moderate, and strict gates.

Strict-gate text-only ROC-AUC fell to 0.8850 for TextCNN and 0.8893 for DeBERTa, but exact-model-aware ROC-AUC remained 0.9904. Thus stricter gating reduces text-only detectability in this bounded matrix while lowering capacity, greatly increasing length, causing completion failures, and retaining a strong model-aware signal. We do not claim an undetectable channel.

\section*{Remaining limitations}

The revision continues to acknowledge the absence of a human participant evaluation, real-world deployment testing, exhaustive attackers, broad quantization coverage, and larger or proprietary model evaluation. The human-authored controls cover a bounded Dolly subset; strict deduplication cannot detect every semantic paraphrase; low-FPR estimates remain unavailable where negative counts are insufficient; entropy thresholds and paired quantization results are limited to the tested matrices. The paired Q4/Q8 visible-recovery comparison was computed from existing Q4 records and the already completed V3 Q8 handoff without generating new Q4 covers, and its exact McNemar \(p=0.1582\) does not support a significant Q8 advantage.

We believe these focused changes directly answer the editor and reviewer while preserving the paper's central negative findings and avoiding a broader rewrite.
