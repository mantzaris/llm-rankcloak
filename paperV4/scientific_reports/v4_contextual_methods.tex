\subsection*{Intact-message contextual acceptability}\label{sec:v4-contextual-method}\label{sec:v4-boundary-method}
The primary coherence evaluation asks whether a complete delivered message is understandable in its authentic prompt context, allowing minor awkwardness. This follow-up was designed after inspecting the earlier fragment results and the author's informal reading. It was frozen before its own final scoring, not retrospectively preregistered as part of the original experiment. The informal reading supplies no participant data. Structured language-model evaluation motivates a reproducible rubric\cite{Liu2023GEval}, while human evaluation requires its own design and evidence\cite{VanDerLee2019BestPractices}. Our local-model study does not reproduce G-Eval's GPT-4 results or validate human perception.

The source population contains 8,280 separately delivered covers in 1,440 segmented trials from 240 payloads in four hexadecimal classes. Each cover is evaluated independently with its actual prompt. Rotating-topic covers are not concatenated into a conversation. From the 37 payloads per class outside the earlier 92-payload scored cohort, a seeded SHA256 rule selects 12 per class. All three generators and both schedules are retained. Two covers per trial are selected by a second identity hash, giving 48 payloads, 288 trials and 576 intact messages. Inclusion probabilities are 12/37 for an outside-cohort payload and \(2/N\) for a message in a trial containing \(N\) covers. The earlier scored stratum has zero inclusion probability. This sample represents the declared outside-cohort stratum rather than an unbiased census of all 240 payloads. No text-quality score, word minimum or evaluator-boundary alignment enters selection. All selected visible texts are available.

Each message is paired with ordinary generation from the same pinned generator and exact prompt-token context. The ordinary arm replaces the eight payload-forced positions with eight greedy positions and then applies the same \code{safe_text_filter_v1} and greedy \code{dynamic_completion_v1} continuation. Both arms have an assigned 264-token cap. The historical heuristic checks the tail after at least eight tail tokens and allows at most 256. Neither arm adds an explicit EOS stop beyond the mask and stopping heuristic. Early heuristic stops and cap-censored outputs are retained, without cutting text to match observed length. The 54 distinct control requests are cached with every pairing preserved. Historical temperature-0.8, top-\(p\)-0.95 controls are not the primary baseline. This compares delivered-message conditions, not an isolated causal effect of a hidden boundary.

Each item is judged separately by the other two model families among pinned Llama 3 8B Instruct, Mistral 7B Instruct v0.3 and Qwen 2.5 7B Instruct, all at Q4\_K\_M. The embedded chat templates receive identical instructions and anonymously identified JSON data containing only the authentic prompt and complete visible text. Punctuation, whitespace, Markdown and errors are preserved as plain text. Payloads, generator identity, source condition, boundaries and prior scores are not shown. Execution is randomized within seeded model blocks. Decoding is deterministic with structured JSON, initially capped at 128 tokens. One same-prompt retry with a 192-token cap is allowed only for invalid or truncated output, retaining both attempts. Missing scores are never accepted by default.

The rubric accepts minor grammar errors, mild repetition and unusual but interpretable wording. It asks judges neither to proofread nor to mentally repair a substantial breakdown. Disruption levels are 0 for clear meaning, 1 for minor awkwardness with readily understandable meaning, 2 for a material disruption or contradiction, and 3 for largely unintelligible or severely disconnected text. Primary acceptance is level 0 or 1. A secondary logical-connectedness score ranges from 1, largely disconnected, to 5, readily connected. It is not another acceptance gate. Short single-idea messages are assessed for contextual fit and intelligibility. Judges may give a brief explanation and, for levels 2 or 3, a literal interfering passage. No extended reasoning is requested.

A separate set of 18 constructed examples contains six clear messages, six understandable messages with minor imperfections and six substantive disruptions. Intended classes were fixed before calibration. The declared sanity check requires acceptance of at least four minor cases and rejection of at least four disrupted cases, with nonconstant output. This is an instrumentation check, not human validation. The final sample, rubric, model pins, analysis and retry policy were frozen after calibration and before judging. Exact-input caching preserves all 2,304 logical judge assignments while reducing execution to 1,182 requests.

For each condition, the panel averages the two assigned judges equally. Messages are averaged within trial and trials within payload before the overall mean. The paired estimate subtracts the ordinary mean from the encoded mean. The same 2,000 payload-cluster bootstrap draws, stratified by artifact class, are used across conditions, generator groups and judges. Intervals condition on the fixed greedy controls. Physical-judge summaries are reported separately because judge and generator assignments are not fully crossed. Complete-pair estimates require both judges in both conditions; all-slot missing-response bounds accompany them. Disagreement, four-level distributions, lengths and literal quotations mapped back to boundaries are secondary descriptive evidence. Neither agreement nor a zero-crossing interval establishes human indistinguishability or equivalence. Supplementary Note S20 gives the full rubric, calibration and subgroup results. The earlier fragment pilots and local conditional-likelihood analysis remain in Supplementary Note S17.
